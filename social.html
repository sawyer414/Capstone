Chapter 01

The Social
Cost of AI

From classrooms to hospitals, AI is disrupting how we learn, communicate, and trust one another — often with consequences we never anticipated.

Education & Truth

PLAGIARISM & MISINFORMATION

When AI Does Your Homework

Schools across the country are grappling with students using generative AI to write essays, complete assignments, and pass off machine-written work as their own. AI plagiarism detectors that once caught this behavior are becoming increasingly unreliable, making it easier to deceive educators.

This isn't just an academic integrity issue — it's shaping how young people learn, think, and develop critical skills. When AI handles the thinking, the learning doesn't happen.

Beyond classrooms, AI-generated misinformation has become a societal threat. Fake news is harder to identify than ever before, and AI-generated images or quotes can destroy a person's reputation in minutes.

AI Plagiarism Detectors Failing
Tools designed to catch AI-written text are increasingly unable to keep up with newer models, creating a detection gap in education.
Deepfakes & Reputation Damage
AI can generate convincing fake images or statements attributed to real people, spreading across social media before corrections can be made.
Bias Baked into Systems
AI trained on biased data can perpetuate and amplify those biases in outputs, causing harm to marginalized groups in ways that are difficult to detect or audit.

"Images of otherwise good people are being ruined due to one or two people online who have access to some form of generative AI."

Privacy & Security

WHO'S PROTECTING YOUR DATA?

AI in Medicine: A Double-Edged Scalpel

There is a growing debate about whether AI should be trusted in medical settings. While AI-assisted diagnostics can process data faster than any human doctor, it cannot understand how different patients experience pain, emotion, or fear.

Patient privacy is a serious concern. Medical data is among the most sensitive information a person has, and AI systems — by their nature — require massive data inputs. Who has access to that data, and how is it protected?

On the cybersecurity front, AI can learn and adapt faster than human security teams can respond. Criminal organizations are weaponizing AI for cyber attacks against individuals, businesses, and governments — creating an arms race that humans may not be able to win.

Medical Privacy at Risk
AI in healthcare requires vast amounts of personal health data, raising serious concerns about how securely that data is stored and who can access it.
AI-Powered Cyber Attacks
Criminals use AI to conduct sophisticated attacks on governments and companies, and AI's ability to learn makes it increasingly difficult to defend against.
National Security Threats
AI-powered digital terrorism poses an unprecedented threat to government infrastructure and national security systems around the world.
Broader Society

FOUR KEY CONCERNS

01

Isolation & Reduced Social Skills

People of all ages are spending more time interacting with chatbots than with other humans, leading to reduced social skills and deeper feelings of isolation and loneliness.

02

Ethical Decision-Making

When AI is used in high-stakes decisions involving human life — medical diagnoses, legal judgments, criminal risk assessments — questions of ethics and accountability become impossible to ignore.

03

Human Bias in AI Code

AI systems are built by humans, and human biases inevitably enter the code. This can lead to discriminatory outputs in hiring, lending, policing, and more — often invisibly.

04

Public Distrust

Research from the Pew Research Center shows that most Americans are more concerned than excited about AI's growing role in daily life, especially regarding jobs and the spread of misinformation.
