Chapter 03

Regulation &
Solutions

The problems caused by unchecked AI are real — but they're not inevitable. Smarter regulation, greater accountability, and civic action can change the trajectory.

Current State

WHERE WE STAND TODAY

⚠️

The United States has no comprehensive federal AI law

As of 2025, the US still lacks a single national framework governing AI development or corporate usage. Over 1,000 AI-related bills have been introduced across state legislatures, but only a small fraction have become law — leaving a critical regulatory gap as the technology accelerates.

What must be done

SIX ESSENTIAL REGULATIONS

01

Caps on Data Center Resource Consumption

Government should mandate limits on how much electricity and water AI data centers can consume. This would force companies to build more efficient systems while protecting local communities from utility price spikes and grid failures.

Environmental
02

Limits on AI's Share of the Workforce

Rules restricting how much of a company's workforce can be replaced by AI would slow the pace of job displacement, giving workers, educators, and governments time to adapt.

Labor
03

Mandatory AI Development Review Before Release

More regulation could fund thorough safety reviews before AI systems reach the public, reducing data leaks, security vulnerabilities, and the release of biased or dangerous models.

Safety
04

Anti-Monopoly Oversight of AI Companies

With OpenAI capturing over 40% of the market and seeking to acquire competitors, antitrust regulators must take AI market concentration as seriously as they would in any other industry.

Market
05

Strict Penalties for AI-Generated Misinformation

Legal consequences for deliberately spreading AI-generated fake news, deepfakes, or falsified content targeting individuals would deter the misuse of generative AI as a reputation-destruction weapon.

Social
06

Data Privacy Standards in Medical AI

Any AI used in healthcare settings must meet strict data protection requirements, with patients given full transparency about how their information is used and stored.

Healthcare
Expected outcomes

WHAT REGULATION WOULD ACHIEVE

Socially

More oversight would give the public time to understand AI before it becomes unavoidable. Most Americans are currently more concerned than excited about AI — slow, transparent rollouts and meaningful safety reviews could rebuild trust.

Accountability for fake news and deepfakes would protect individuals from having their lives ruined by AI-generated content, and clearer guidelines in schools could address the plagiarism crisis before it hollows out education.

Economically

Limits on workforce AI replacement would slow the pace of job displacement, allowing economies to adjust rather than crash. Communities near data centers would benefit from resource limits that stop utility prices from spiraling out of control.

Antitrust action against near-monopoly AI companies would restore competition, drive innovation, and ensure that the benefits of AI technology aren't captured entirely by a handful of corporations.

Civic Action
